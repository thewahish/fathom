The Product That Spins Before It Stretches

Multiplying two complex numbers is usually taught as FOIL-ing four terms and remembering that i² = -1. There's a picture underneath that needs no algebra at all: to multiply A by B, turn A by however far around the plane B itself points, then stretch it by however long B itself is. Drag either arrow and the product traces exactly that — a spin, then a stretch, nothing else.

The teal arrow is A, the indigo arrow is B — both plain vectors from the origin, both complex numbers. To find A × B, don't multiply components: rotate A by B's own angle (the small indigo arc, and the dashed teal arrow it lands on) and stretch that by B's own length (the solid dark arrow, reaching further out — or pulling back in — along that same line). The result's angle is just ∠A + ∠B added together, and its length is just |A| × |B| — multiplication of complex numbers is rotation composed with scaling, and B alone is the instruction for both. Drag either amber tip. Try ×i: B has length exactly 1, so the "stretch" step does nothing — the dashed arrow and the product land on top of each other, and multiplying by i is only a 90° turn. Try ×2: B's angle is exactly 0, so there's no turn at all, only a stretch.
