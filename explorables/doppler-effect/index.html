The Wave That Piles Up Ahead of You

A siren doesn't change pitch. The air doesn't care how fast the siren is moving. Every ripple it sends out still travels at exactly the same speed in every direction — which is precisely why a moving source sounds different coming and going.

Every ring here is born from the exact spot the source was in when it fired, then grows outward at the same fixed speed, c — the source's own motion never gets added to it. So each new ring starts a little closer to the ring ahead of it (source chasing its own waves) and a little farther from the ring behind it (source running from them). Bunched-up rings arrive more often per second than they were sent — a higher pitch. Spread-out rings arrive less often — a lower one. Nothing about the wave itself changed; only where it started from moved. Drag the amber lever past 1.00c and watch the front listener stop hearing anything ahead of the source at all — the rings can no longer get out in front of something moving faster than they travel, so instead they stack into a single V-shaped shock front trailing behind it. That's every sonic boom, and the reason a source right at the threshold sounds like nothing, then everything at once.
