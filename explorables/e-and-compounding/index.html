No. 05

The Number Compound Interest Can't Get Past

Compound $1 at 100% interest more often — yearly, monthly, daily, every hour — and it grows a little more every time. Drag the frequency up as far as it goes. The growth doesn't run away. It walks straight up to a wall and stops just short of it.

12×/yr
2.613035
0.105247

Split the year into n equal chunks and pay 1/n of the 100% rate at each one — that's ordinary compound interest, and the year-end value is (1 + 1/n)n. Push n up and every chunk adds a smaller top-up, but there are more of them; those two effects don't cancel, they trade off, so the total creeps up and then all but stops moving. On the right, the staircase is exactly that trade-off made visible: n discrete jumps across the year, chasing the dashed smooth curve without ever quite becoming it. Drag the amber dot on the left curve all the way to the right — splitting the year into 3,000 pieces (compounding every few hours) still only reaches 2.71828…, the same wall every time, no matter how far you push. That wall is e. It isn't a coincidence — it's the definition: e is what compounding becomes once you squeeze the interval to nothing, which is why it's the base rate underneath every process that grows continuously, from bacteria to radioactive decay to the natural logarithm itself.
