The Directions a Matrix Doesn't Turn

A matrix grabs every arrow in the plane and sends it somewhere else — stretched, sheared, spun. Drag one arrow all the way around a full circle and watch its image swing around too. Almost everywhere it points somewhere new. A couple of directions, though, refuse to turn at all — they just get longer or shorter, dead on the same line. Those are the eigenvectors.

The teal arrow is v, wherever you drag it on the circle. The indigo arrow is Av — the same vector, run through the current matrix. Sweep v all the way around and Av mostly points somewhere else entirely: the matrix is turning space, not just stretching it. But watch the "Av vs v" readout as you sweep — at one or two exact angles, marked by dashed lines through the origin, it drops to 0° (or 180°, still the same line): Av lands right back on v's own line, only longer or shorter by a factor called the eigenvalue. Those angles are the matrix's eigenvectors — the only directions it doesn't turn, just scales. Drag the amber tip around the circle. Try rotation: a pure spin has no direction that survives it, so the readout never touches 0° no matter where you drag — every arrow gets turned by the same fixed angle. Shear only has one surviving direction, not two.
