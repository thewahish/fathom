The Bar That Knows Exactly When to Stop

Sorting doesn't need a formula — just one rule, applied over and over: pick up the next bar and slide it left past everything bigger than it, until something stops you. Drag the amber bar as far left as it'll go. Where it locks in is exactly where it belongs.

This is insertion sort's entire inner loop, done by hand instead of by a computer: grab the next unsorted bar and try to drag it into the sorted stretch on the left. Every sorted bar taller than it slides out of the way as you pass; the moment you reach one that's shorter — or run out of bars — you physically can't go any further. That wall isn't decoration, it's the comparison itself: the exact spot where the bar belongs, found by feel, one "is this bigger than me?" at a time. Release before you hit the wall and the bar springs back, still unsorted; drag it all the way and it clicks into place, the sorted stretch grows by one bar, and the next one becomes yours to place. Hit Step to watch one insertion happen automatically, or Run to watch the whole row sort itself, one wall at a time. With 9 bars the worst case is 36 comparisons — Shuffle a few times and watch that number swing between a lucky near-sorted shuffle and an unlucky reversed one.
