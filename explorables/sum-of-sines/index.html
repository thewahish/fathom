No. 12

The Shape That's Secretly a Sum of Sines

A square wave looks nothing like a sine wave. Neither does a sawtooth, or a triangle. And yet each one is just several sine waves, at whole-number multiples of one frequency, added together in the right proportions. Build them yourself, one pure tone at a time.

—

Each lever below is one pure tone — a plain sine wave at harmonic n, n times the frequency of the first one. Push a lever up or down and its tone's own ripple gets added into the big curve above, in real time. That's the entire idea: any repeating shape, no matter how sharp its corners, is some recipe of these — which frequencies, and how loud each one is. Drag the amber levers by hand, or tap a preset to snap eight of them straight to the exact recipe for a square, sawtooth, or triangle wave — then watch how close eight terms gets to the dashed target. Square and sawtooth have sharp corners, so they need more and more high harmonics that never fully die out, leaving a ripple right at the jump (called the Gibbs phenomenon — it's why sharp digital edges pick up ringing when band-limited). Triangle's corners are gentler, so its harmonics fade fast and eight terms already look almost exact. Your ears do this decomposition automatically, all day: it's how one messy pressure wave in the air becomes the separate notes of a chord.
